On weight: no training plan out-trains the diet, so treat the sessions as what keeps the muscle while a modest food deficit does the fat loss. A slow deficit also protects goals 4 and 5 — aggressive cutting takes legs and strength with it. Goal 4 is the one with a live tension: legs and calves are the lagging area, and conditioning volume competes with leg growth. That means leg work gets protected — hard sets first in the session, never cut for time, and the running/rope stays genuinely easy so it doesn't eat the recovery legs need. Grip, calves and trunk get frequency (near-daily) because they tolerate it; squats and lunges get intensity, not frequency.
The 6-Block Session
Every session · 45–60 min · 3–5x/week · after Eugene Teo
▶
#BlockTimeRestYour tools
1Plyometrics3–5 min30–60sJump rope bounces, pogo hops, low box jumps — springy, not tiring
2Power~5 min2 minKB swings, jump squats, explosive pull-ups, clean & press — fast, fresh, low reps
3Strength10–15 min2–3 minHeaviest work: vest-weighted pull-ups/dips, Bulgarian split squat, DB deadlift
4Hypertrophy15–20 min60–90sThe supersets and volume work — existing Upper/Lower sessions live here
5Mobility~5 minminimalRotation is non-negotiable: band woodchoppers, thoracic rotations, cossack squat, dead hang
6The EDC or Cardio5–10 min45–60sEither: farmer's + suitcase carries for time — or rope intervals / ruck / run. Both optional, pick what the day needs
Rest, in one line — the earlier the block, the longer the rest. Power and strength sets exist to be good, not tiring: if you're still breathing hard, you rested too little and the next set will be worse. Hypertrophy is the opposite — 60–90s is enough, and a bit of fatigue is the point. In supersets, 30–45s between the two movements, then a full rest before repeating the pair. On a tight day, cut sets before you cut rest.
From Eugene Teo's hybrid bodybuilding template. The order is the point: springy and fast while the nervous system is fresh, heavy next, pump work after, stretch while warm, engine or carries last. Block 6 is a choice: EDC carries if the day has no other conditioning, cardio if you want the engine work — or both when there's time. Short on time? 30 min = blocks 1-2-3-5. 15 min = blocks 1+5 plus rope. How the rest of the program maps: The 300 and Cindy are benchmark circuits that cover blocks 2–4 and 6 in one hit — bookend them with block 1 before and block 5 after. The rope EMOMs, Quick Hit and rope sessions are standalone block-4/block-6 work for nights and kid days. Zone 2, rucking and sprints are block 6 moved to its own day.

Ten patterns, one session, all from what's in the garage. Run block 3 as supersets — A1/A2 together, then B1/B2 — pull paired with legs so nothing competes. SHORT VERSION (~30 min): blocks 1, 2, 3A, 5. Progression: add vest load to pull-ups and dips before adding reps; add DB weight to squat and RDL; box stays 24" and gets more reps.
Where it comes from — Eugene Teo: the 6-block order and the insistence that rotation never gets skipped. Lee Downing Keat: weighted calisthenics as the engine of the strength block — vest on pull-ups and dips, progressed by load not endless reps; and the principle that a short, well-chosen routine beats a long one. The Bioneer: loaded carries, hangs and grip as trainable qualities in their own right. AthleanX: joint-friendly sequencing and the paused inverted row for scapular health rather than just rowing weight. Bodyweight Muscle: vest-loaded bodyweight over machines, and box jumps you step down from — sustainable for a bloke in his thirties with a job and kids. Mover's Odyssey: quality of movement over rep count — if the pattern degrades, the set is finished.
Grip note — Angles90 sold. Pronated pull-ups are fine meanwhile; rings give you a neutral/rotating grip today if the elbows grumble, since the rings turn as you pull. Worth replacing with a neutral option eventually — it's easier on the elbows and lets you hold heavier vest loads.

Running
Sprints + Zone 2
▶
PhaseWeeksDetail
A · Walk-run1–425–30 min: run 1 min easy / walk 90s, x8–10. Build toward run 3 / walk 1. 2–3x/week
B · Continuous5–820–30 min unbroken easy pace + strides (4 x 15s fast-but-relaxed) after one run a week
Zone 2ongoing30–45 min conversational — the base under everything
Complete-novice progression — the classic mistake is running too fast too soon. Every Phase A–B run should be conversational; if you can't speak a sentence, slow down. This card is the aerobic engine only — sprinting is its own practice with its own card below. DISTANCE CEILING: ~10k / 60 min is the max this program ever asks for. The goal is Hyrox-style fitness and growing Zone 2 time, not racing distance — progress is measured in comfortable minutes, not kilometres. (Hyrox is 8 x 1km, so a 10k ceiling more than covers it.) If you've never enjoyed running, odds are you've only run too fast — every easy run here should feel almost boringly comfortable. Half marathon stays an option someday if you surprise yourself, but nothing here trains toward it. IMPACT BUDGET: easy running and jump rope both load the calves and shins — treat them as one bucket. While run volume is building, keep the rope volume cap and don't ramp both in the same week. One new impact stressor at a time.

Sprints — Hill First
Own track, separate from running · 1–2x/week
▶
PhaseWeeksDetail
1 · Learn1–34 x ~8s uphill at 60–70% effort. Walk down + stand until fully recovered (2+ min). 1x/week
2 · Build4–85–6 x 10s uphill at 80–85%. Full recovery. 1–2x/week
3 · Sharpen9+6–8 x 10–15s uphill at 90%+, or transition to flat: 4–6 accelerations building over 30–40m
Watch: Beginner's guide to hill sprints (Bodyweight Muscle) and why sprint at all (Mover's Odyssey). This is a POWER practice, not conditioning — it lives in blocks 1–2 of the template, done fresh, never to failure. Stop the session the moment speed or form drops. Why hills: the slope forces good mechanics (forward lean, high knee drive), shortens the stride so hamstrings can't overreach, and softens impact — which is why this track can start NOW, alongside walk-run Phase A and rope, without blowing the impact budget. Flat sprinting waits until Phase 3. Rules: full warm-up plus strides first, 48h+ after heavy leg work, and never the day after nights.

The goal: fit enough to do a Hyrox without ever entering one. Hyrox is 8 x 1km runs alternating with stations (sled, carries, sandbag lunges, wall balls, burpees, row/ski) — your program already covers the strength half with vest lunges, KB swings and daily carries. The missing skill is COMPROMISED RUNNING: running on legs that are already working. Don't start this until running Phase B is comfortable; then run it every 1–2 weeks in place of a sprint day and score total time. Zone 2 base + this session = a Hyrox-shaped engine.
The EDC
Every Day Carry + calves · daily add-on
▶
MovementSets
Farmer's Carry (both hands)2 x 30–45s
Suitcase Carry (one hand, swap)2 x 30s each side
Calf Raises — slow, full stretch2 x 12–20
Done every day or nearly every day, on top of whatever else the day holds. All carries are for TIME, not distance — small gym and garden, so the clock is the honest measure. Progress by adding seconds first, then load. The suitcase carry is the quiet gem — one-sided load forces the trunk to resist side-bend, so it's core work disguised as carrying shopping. CALVES: this is where they'll actually grow — frequency beats intensity for calves, and they recover fast enough to hit near-daily. Non-negotiables: full stretch at the bottom with a 1-second pause, slow up, no bouncing. Off a step is better than flat floor. Alternate straight-leg (gastroc) and bent-knee/seated (soleus) across the week. Add load once 2 x 20 is easy. Whole thing is ~7 minutes.

Rope Setup
Sized for 193–194 cm · read first
▶
RopeJobLength
Elite Jumps beadedLearning · rhythm feedbackFull 10 ft — don't trim yet
Cardio 2.0 Weighted (1/4lb)Conditioning · outdoor OKFull 10 ft — don't trim yet
Both are 10 ft adjustable cords — at your height that's right at spec, so start both at full length. Beginner check: stand on the middle of the rope, handles should reach armpit height, and the rope should clear your head by 25–45 cm while jumping. Good news on the Cardio 2.0 Weighted: the ~130g / 6mm PVC cord is beginner-friendly — the extra weight slows rotation and lets you feel where the rope is, like the beads do. So use either from day one: beaded indoors (best feedback, but beads chew up on rough ground), weighted PVC outdoors (fair warning — it stings more on misses). After 3–4 weeks of consistent timing, shorten both gradually — beads out one or two at a time, PVC trimmed — until handles hit mid-chest. Never cut early; you can't add length back. Jump on a mat, wood or rubber, not bare concrete (better for your calves and the cords).

These drills mirror Lauren's beginner video (linked in Study Material) — watch it first, then run this as your checklist. Form cues: jump 3–5 cm off the floor, land on the balls of your feet with soft knees, elbows pinned to your ribs, hands just in front of your hips, spin from the wrists — not the shoulders. Eyes ahead, not down. Exit test: 25 unbroken bounces. Calves adapt slower than lungs — if they're sore, take a day. That soreness IS the adaptation window for injury, don't push through it.

Phase 3 — Make It a Weapon
Week 5+ · conditioning tool unlocked
▶

Learn the boxer skip (weight shifts side to side) and alternate-foot step early in this phase — they halve the impact per leg and are what make 15+ minute sessions sustainable. The weighted Cardio 2.0 shines here — the 1/4lb adds real conditioning load over long intervals; keep the beaded rope for learning new footwork (side swings, then criss-cross). Rope sessions sit in the same slot as Sprints/Zone 2 — hard intervals count as a sprint day, easy freestyle counts as Zone 2. Fine on kid days, between or after nights.

Stay Unbroken
Injury prevention · non-negotiable
▶
RuleDetail
SurfaceMat, wood or rubber — never bare concrete
Land quietBalls of feet, soft knees, 3–5 cm hops. If you can hear your landings, they're too hard
Calf prepCalf raises 2 x 15 on non-rope days (already a finisher option — pick it)
Stretch afterCalf stretch (straight + bent knee) 2 x 30s each side post-session
48h ruleShin or calf PAIN (not soreness) = 48h off the rope. Ruck or Zone 2 instead
Volume capDon't add more than ~5 min of jumping per week
Watch: How To Avoid Pain & Injury — Lauren Jumps (Start Here Pt. 5). Shin splints are the classic beginner exit — they come from concrete, heavy landings, tight calves and ramping volume too fast, and ignored they progress toward stress fractures. At 190+ cm every landing carries more force than it does for smaller jumpers, so these rules matter more for you, not less. General soreness is fine and expected; sharp or localized shin pain is a stop sign.
